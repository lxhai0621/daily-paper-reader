Abstract 22 

Summary: The Clinical Interpretation of Variants in Cancer (CIViC) knowledgebase provides a 23 

community -driven, open -source platform for discussing the biological and clinical signifi cance 24 

of molecular variants in cancer. To enable users to make complex connections between CIViC 25 

information, we developed the CIViC Model Context Protocol (MCP) server, which allows users 26 

to interface with the CIViC API through natural language via large language models (LLMs), 27 

facilitating the rapid summarization of expertly curated cancer variant interpretations. 28 

Availability and implementation: The CIViC MCP server is detailed at 29 

https://github.com/griffithlab/civic -mcp -server . The repository includes instructions for 30 

accessing the server through the Claude desktop app (our recommended approach; 31 

Supplementary Figure 1) and hosting it locally with GPT -5, as well as a Python script for 32 

directly querying the MCP server. We also provide an MCP -supported Chatbot for CIViC users 33 

at  https://civicdb.org/mcp -chat .34 

Supplemental information: Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics Advances 35 

online. 36

1. Introduction 42 

The identification of molecular variants drives precision oncology, informing clinical decision -43 

making and treatment strategies. To give just one illustrative example, ALK fusion variants 44 

occur in fewer than 5% of non -small cell lung cancers but can be targeted by kinase inhibitors, 45 

such as crizotinib, for signifi cant improvements in patient outcomes  (Kwak  et al.  2010) . The 46 

complex mutational landscape of cancers makes the development of molecular variant 47 

knowledgebases critical to the goal of enabling the use of genomic information in precision 48 

oncology. To address this, the Clinical Interpretation of Variants in Cancer (CIViC) 49 

knowledgebase provides a community -driven, open -source platform for assessing the importance 50 

of molecular variants in cancer (available at  civicdb.org ) (Griffith  et al.  2017; Danos  et al.  2019; 51 

Krysiak  et al.  2022) . CIViC curates Evidence Items from peer‑reviewed primary literature as its 52 

fundamental unit of information. Each item is assigned an Evidence Type that reflects the 53 

variant’s clinical or biological effect, including diagnostic, predictive, prognostic, predisposing, 54 

oncogenic, or functional. Each Evidence Type has a corresponding signifi cance. For example, 55 

predictive evidence can indicate whether a tumor is sensitive or resistant to a specific therapy. 56 

CIViC Assertions are created to summarize collections of Evidence Items within a specific 57 

cancer context, adhering to recognized guidelines  (Li  et al.  2017; Horak  et al.  2022) .58 

LLMs provide a natural -language interface to CIViC, enabling users to discover, aggregate, and 59 

summarize curated oncology knowledge without navigating to multiple pages. They excel at 60 

transforming unstructured biomedical text into structured outputs through few - or zero -shot 61 

learning and entity normalization. However, LLMs cannot guarantee coverage of specialized, 62 

rapidly updated resources like CIViC from pretraining alone, and they may misinterpret CIViC’s 63 

data model, fabricating details or citations when asked for fine -grained clinical information 64

based web retrieval, identifying relevant pages through a search engine and then reading their 66 

contents. As a result, discoverability is constrained by the effectiveness of the search mechanism. 67 

Agentic web or UI interaction offers a different form of indirect access, in which the model 68 

navigates websites by simulating human inputs. However, for a highly structured resource such 69 

as CIViC, it remains unclear whether this approach can match the accuracy, provenance, and 70 

efficiency of direct structured retrieval. In this work, we evaluate whether direct, structured API 71 

access through a standardized protocol can close this gap, improving both accuracy and 72 

efficiency over unassisted and agent -based approaches. Additionally, because CIViC’s curation 73 

is intentionally granular, captured in individual evidence items for transparency and provenance, 74 

many users may benefit from a conversational layer that retrieves the right items and composes 75 

succinct, well -cited answers. To provide this layer, we developed a Model Context Protocol 76 

(MCP) server that allows LLMs to query CIViC information seamlessly and provide detailed 77 

answers to users (Figure 1). MCP servers expose external tools and data sources through a 78 

simple, standardized interface, enabling LLMs to issue user -guided queries and receive 79 

structured results. In biomedicine, MCP servers are increasingly wrapping domain APIs, 80 

databases, and literature search endpoints  (Kuehl  et al.  2025) . The CIViC MCP server benefits 81 

both new and experienced users by supporting complex, reproducible queries and rapid, citation -82 

rich summaries. 83 

The CIViC MCP server leverages CIViC’s public GraphQL API to facilitate user -guided 84 

queries. The GraphQL schema makes the API straightforward for LLMs to use as objects are 85 

explicitly linked (e.g., Evidence Item → Molecular Profile → Variant), letting models see how to 86 

move from a clinical question to the exact fields it needs. Through introspection, the LLM reads 87

precise meanings specific to the platform and may be misinterpreted by an LLM  (Krysiak  et al. 91 

2022) . Despite GraphQL introspection, early tests revealed that Claude Sonnet 4 frequently mis -92 

specified types and arguments, leading to invalid or overly broad queries. While introspection 93 

did improve reliability on later queries, where the LLM could contextualize GraphQL error 94 

messages against the schema and refine its requests, it did not close the gap relative to predefined 95 

tools. We circumvent this challenge in the current implementation of the CIViC MCP server by 96 

predefining the GraphQL queries, rather than allowing an LLM to write them to ensure 97 

reliability. We note that these challenges are not specific to GraphQL. A REST API would 98 

similarly require the LLM to know the correct endpoints, parameter names, and expected values, 99 

information that is available in documentation but that current LLMs cannot reliably 100 

operationalize without a structured tool interface. 101 

2. CIViC MCP Server 102 

The CIViC MCP server is hosted on Cloudflare and is publicly accessible. It provides two tools 103 

based on predefined GraphQL queries: one for retrieving CIViC Evidence Items and one for 104 

retrieving CIViC Assertions. For both tools, a Molecular Profile name is required, while cancer 105 

type and therapy may be provided as optional filters to further narrow the results. The Evidence 106 

Item tool retrieves curated information specific to individual studies, including an assessment of 107 

evidence strength, a summary of study findings within the relevant clinical context, and 108 

associated PubMed IDs. The Assertion tool retrieves higher -level summaries that synthesize 109 

collections of Evidence Items within a specific cancer -variant context. These tools were made 110

separate to accommodate questions requiring different levels of granularity. Each response 111 

includes the relevant CIViC URLs, enabling direct verification of CIViC -specific claims 112 

generated by the LLM. Evidence Item responses also include the associated PubMed ID for 113 

validation against the source literature. To support accurate summarization, the MCP server 114 

additionally returns definitions for each CIViC field. 115 

To handle spelling differences and alternate names, the MCP server normalizes user inputs 116 

(molecular profiles, cancer types, therapies) to the closest CIViC preferred label. The preferred 117 

label is the canonical, API -recognized name used for exact matching. This normalization relies 118 

on precomputed alias lists that were generated from external and CIViC -curated resources, rather 119 

than querying those resources at runtime. For gene -level aliases, we used the VICC Gene 120 

Normalization Service to assemble synonym lists for genes appearing in CIViC molecular 121 

profiles  (Kuzma, Stevenson and Wagner 2025) . Variant aliases were obtained from CIViC’s 122 

curated alias fields. Cancer type aliases were derived from the Disease Ontology, and therapy 123 

aliases were derived from the NCI Thesaurus  (Sioutos  et al.  2007; Schriml  et al.  2012) . The 124 

MCP server scores candidates with Dice’s coefficient over character bigrams and selects the 125 

highest -scoring alias. We chose the Dice -Sørensen coefficient because it is fast and 126 

memory‑light, making it a good fit for Cloudflare Workers despite more complex matchers 127 

offering higher accuracy. 128

3. Benchmarking Accuracy and Efficiency of LLM Retrieval Methods for CIViC 142 

We selected a controlled classification task: given a CIViC triplet (molecular profile, cancer 143 

type, therapy), the LLM must determine which CIViC clinical signifi cance has supporting 144 

evidence. To assess how effectively LLMs can retrieve and interpret current CIViC information 145 

through the MCP server, we compared GPT -5’s performance with and without the MCP server 146 

on the same set of 100 randomly selected triplets from CIViC Evidence Items. We used a zero -147 

shot prompt at temperature = 1 (Supplementary Prompt 1). We prompted GPT -5 to determine, 148 

for each candidate CIViC clinical signifi cance, whether the available evidence supported that 149 

signifi cance, did not support it, or provided no evidence for it. These three outcomes served as 150 

the class labels for evaluation, allowing us to distinguish between positive evidence, 151 

contradictory evidence, and absence of evidence. In CIViC, signifi cance labels are grouped 152 

within Evidence Types, which broadly indicate the type of clinical or biological association 153 

described by an Evidence Item’s clinical summary. Each triplet could have multiple associated 154 

Evidence Types and signifi cances. Performance was evaluated at the signifi cance -label level and 155

substantially improves classification performance without adding measurable latency. 168 

4. Conclusion 169 

We demonstrate that MCP servers provide standardized, LLM -friendly gateways to curated 170 

biomedical databases, enabling precise retrieval and structured summarization. Connecting 171 

CIViC to LLMs through MCP improves the accuracy of extracting clinical signifi cance in 172 

variant -disease -therapy contexts compared with using an LLM’s web client, and it reduces 173 

latency relative to agent -mode browsing while maintaining fidelity. By offering structured, real -174 

time access to curated oncology knowledge, the CIViC MCP server mitigates gaps in pretraining 175 

coverage, reduces hallucination risk, and yields reproducible, citation -rich outputs. MCP -176 

mediated access both improves performance and shortens time to answer. Future work will 177 

develop constrained query -composition methods that complement predefined tools, enabling the 178
