Abstract 

Large language models (LLMs) have shown strong performance across various tasks, but they still struggle with questions involving eth-ical judgment. Previous studies have attempted to train LLMs on ethical standards, but the di-versity and relativity of ethical norms make them difficult to fully internalize in model pa-rameters. As an alternative, we introduce nor-mative RAG , a retrieval-augmented approach that supports ethical judgment using external normative knowledge. Normative retrieval in-volves a distinct asymmetry between context-rich narrative queries and generalized nor-mative statements. Existing factual retrieval methods rely on query-only expansion into a document-like form, making them insuffi-cient for resolving this asymmetry. Therefore, we propose Intermediate Representation-based 

Embedding Alignment ( IREA ), a bidirectional alignment method that maps both text types into a shared situation-behavior representation. This representation captures ethically salient contextual and behavioral information in a nor-malized form, reducing surface-level discrepan-cies and improving alignment in the embedding space. Experimental results show that IREA im-proves normative retrieval and downstream eth-ical judgment across multiple settings, demon-strating the effectiveness of bidirectional align-ment for normative RAG. 

1 Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) rely on paramet-ric knowledge acquired during pretraining, which can lead to hallucinations when the required infor-mation is not sufficiently represented in their pa-rameters (Minaee et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025a; Park et al., 2025). Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) mitigates this limitation by leveraging ex-ternal knowledge to improve response reliability (Lewis et al., 2021; Gao et al., 2024; Ram et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2026). Existing RAG systems have 

Figure 1: Comparison of factual and normative RAG examples. Same-colored highlights indicate semantic correspondences between texts. 

primarily focused on retrieving factual knowledge, which we refer to as factual RAG .However, LLM limitations extend beyond factu-ality and also arise in questions requiring ethical judgment (Senthilkumar et al., 2024; Tao et al., 2024). Previous studies have improved the ethical capabilities of LLMs through fine-tuning on syn-thetic datasets (Li et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2022). Yet ethical standards are diverse, context-dependent, and evolving, making them difficult to fully inter-nalize in model parameters. Moreover, frequent parameter updates to reflect changing norms may degrade the model’s general capabilities (Wang et al., 2024c; Huang et al., 2025b; Imanov, 2026). To address these limitations, we introduce nor-mative RAG , which supports ethical judgment by retrieving relevant rules of thumb (RoTs) from ex-ternal sources. However, as shown in Figure 1, nor-mative retrieval is more challenging than factual 1arXiv:2610.04974v1 [cs.CL] 4 Oct 2026 retrieval because queries and RoTs differ substan-tially in representation. In factual retrieval, queries often share key terms, such as event names or proper nouns, with relevant documents that pro-vide direct evidence for the answer. This makes semantic similarity-based retrieval relatively effec-tive. In contrast, normative retrieval requires match-ing concrete narrative scenarios to abstract norma-tive principles. In this example, multiple behaviors in the story collectively imply ‘rudeness’ , while the corresponding RoT expresses the norm at a more abstract level. Thus, the connection between the story and the RoT is often latent rather than lexical. Existing factual retrieval methods address query-document mismatch by expanding underspecified queries into document-like representations. Such expansion is effective when additional information improves query-document alignment. Normative retrieval, however, exhibits a distinct asymmetry. The query is already a context-rich narrative, while the RoT is a concise and abstract normative state-ment. Transforming the query directly into an RoT-like form may over-compress the narrative and dis-card information needed to identify the underlying moral issue shared by the query and the RoT. To this end, we propose Intermediate 

Representation-based Embedding Alignment (IREA ), a bidirectional alignment approach that maps both queries and RoTs into a shared situation-behavior representation. In this representation, 

situation specifies the context in which a behavior is interpreted, while behavior specifies the action being judged, recommended, or prohibited. Motivated by Frame Analysis (Goffman, 1974), IREA builds on the view that an action derives its meaning from the contextual frame in which it is interpreted. For example, refusing a request may be appropriate in one context but rude or inappropriate in another. By representing both queries and RoTs in terms of their situation-behavior relations, IREA aligns them at the level of moral meaning while reducing surface-level differences in wording and narrative structure. This structure improves their alignment in the embedding space without discarding information necessary for ethical judgment. Experimental results show that IREA improves RoT retrieval and enhances downstream ethical judgment without additional training. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce normative RAG, which extends RAG beyond factual knowledge retrieval to support ethical judgment in LLMs. • We propose IREA, which aligns narrative sto-ries and abstract RoTs through a shared inter-mediate representation to reduce semantic and structural mismatches in normative retrieval. • We show that IREA consistently improves RoT retrieval and downstream ethical judg-ment across benchmark datasets, demonstrat-ing the effectiveness of retrieval-based access to external normative knowledge. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 LLM Ethics 

Recent LLMs have become more ethically reliable through safety alignment, but they still struggle with complex ethical contexts (Fareed et al., 2025; Hung et al., 2023; Hui et al., 2025; Jiashen et al., 2025). They often miss subtle contextual variations, leading to formulaic responses that overlook rel-evant ethical considerations (Soffer et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2024). Moreover, they insufficiently ac-count for cultural diversity and the relativity of ethical standards, resulting in culturally misaligned responses (Durmus et al., 2023; AlKhamissi et al., 2024). They tend to be biased toward Western-centric values and fail to capture diverse ethical expectations across cultures (Shi et al., 2024; Pham et al., 2024; Dwivedi et al., 2025). Previous studies have primarily enhanced the ethical alignment of LLMs through fine-tuning on synthetic datasets (Dwivedi et al., 2023; Chan et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2025). Some studies enrich train-ing data with additional supervision. Kabir et al. (2025) incorporated explicit reasoning processes, while Liu et al. (2025) used role-play data to help models learn cultural norms. Other approaches use ethical principles or human values as reward signals in reinforcement learning (Tennant et al., 2025; Dai et al., 2023; Chittepu et al., 2025). Training-based methods can improve ethical alignment with a fixed set of norms, but adapting models to diverse ethical contexts through repeated retraining is inefficient. We therefore propose a RAG-based approach that enables norm-grounded ethical responses without additional training. 

2.2 RAG 

RAG has demonstrated strong performance across knowledge-intensive tasks, but its effectiveness de-2Figure 2: Normative retrieval process of IREA. 

pends heavily on the relevance of retrieved docu-ments (Cuconasu et al., 2024; Zhuang et al., 2024). To address this limitation, various retriever train-ing methods have been proposed. DPR (Karpukhin et al., 2020), Contriever (Izacard et al., 2022), and E5 (Wang et al., 2024a) employ bi-encoder archi-tectures trained to place relevant query-document pairs closer in the embedding space and irrele-vant ones farther apart. In addition, some meth-ods jointly train a single retriever across diverse retrieval tasks and objectives, yielding more gener-alizable embeddings and greater robustness across query types (Zhang et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024). Nevertheless, real-world queries are often in-complete or ambiguous, making it difficult even for strong retrievers to identify relevant documents based solely on the query. To enrich the query with additional context, Query2Doc (Wang et al., 2023) and HyDE (Gao et al., 2022) generate hypothetical documents for retrieval, while Rewrite-Retrieve-Read (Ma et al., 2023) reformulates the query into a more retrieval-friendly form. However, these ap-proaches are primarily designed for factual knowl-edge retrieval and are less effective for abstract expressions such as norms. To address this limita-tion, we propose a method tailored to normative RAG, where queries and documents differs funda-mentally from those in factual RAG. 

3 Methodology 

3.1 Task Formulation 

Normative RAG aims to retrieve RoTs that are eth-ically relevant to a given query and use them as supporting evidence for ethical judgment. In this setting, the query is a story that requires ethical assessment, and the documents are RoTs that pro-vide normative guidance. 3.2 IREA 

We propose IREA to mitigate the representational gap between stories and RoTs. Unlike existing fac-tual retrieval approaches that reformulate only the query, IREA maps both stories and RoTs into a shared situation-behavior representation. In this schema, situation denotes the contextual conditions under which a behavior is ethically interpreted, while behavior denotes the action being evaluated in a story or prescribed by an RoT. By representing both text types with the same schema, IREA en-ables direct comparison between their correspond-ing situation and behavior components. IREA is inspired by Goffman’s Frame Analysis, which posits that the meaning of an action is con-structed within the contextual frame in which it is interpreted, rather than being inherent in the action itself. Accordingly, the same action can carry dif-ferent social or moral meanings depending on its context. We extend this view to ethical reasoning by conceptualizing ethical judgment as the inter-pretation and evaluation of behavior within a given situation. This motivates IREA to decompose each text into two components: situation, which captures the context of interpretation, and behavior, which captures the action under moral evaluation. Specifically, we use an LLM to extract the sit-uation and behavior components from each story 3and RoT. Baselines We compare IREA against four repre-sentative unsupervised approaches: Naive RAG, HyDE (Gao et al., 2022), Query2doc (Wang et al., 2023), and Rewrite-Retrieve-Read (referred to as Rewrite) (Ma et al., 2023). Naive RAG retrieves documents based on semantic similarity to the query. HyDE, Query2doc, and Rewrite improve retrieval by reformulating or expanding the orig-inal query. HyDE generates a hypothetical docu-ment in a zero-shot setting, while Query2doc gen-erates a pseudo-document in a 4-shot setting and concatenates it with the original query to form an expanded query. Rewrite reformulates the query into a retrieval-friendly form using 3-shot exam-ples. We adapt these methods to normative retrieval using the modified prompts described in Appendix C.2. For a fair comparison, all expanded queries and intermediate representations are generated with GPT-4o-mini-2024-07-18 (Achiam et al., 2023) at a temperature of 0.7. We use E5-base-v2 (Wang et al., 2024b), all-MiniLM-L12-v2 (Wang et al., 2020), LLM2Vec (BehnamGhader et al., 2024) for text em-bedding. For ethical judgment, we use Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (Qwen et al., 2025), Gemma-2-9b-it (Team et al., 2024) and GPT-4o-mini with temper-ature set to 0. Based on retrieval performance, we use E5 in all subsequent experiments. 

Metrics We evaluate retrieval performance using Recall@ k, MRR@10, and nDCG@10. Recall@ k

measures whether the gold RoT appears within the top-k results, where we set k ∈ { 1, 3, 5, 10 } con-sidering the database size. MRR@10 evaluates how highly the gold RoT is ranked based on its recip-rocal rank. nDCG@10 measures overall ranking quality by discounting relevance scores according to rank. We evaluate ethical judgment performance using Accuracy and F1 score. 

4.2 Normative Retrieval Performance 

Table 1 shows that IREA consistently outper-forms the baselines across experimental settings. Compared with HyDE, the strongest baseline, IREA with E5 increases Recall@10 by 11.42 and 11.50%p on Scruples and NormAd, respectively. With MiniLM, IREA also yields consistent gains across all metrics on both datasets. With LLM2Vec, IREA achieves the best performance on Scruples, although the improvement over the baselines is smaller on NormAd. Overall, IREA demonstrates robust performance across embedding models and datasets, while baseline performance varies across embedding models. These results suggest that map-ping both stories and RoTs into a shared intermedi-ate representation is more effective than query-only reformulation because it aligns them through com-parable semantic structures. Appendix A further shows that IREA remains robust to variations in IR generator capacity, prompt formulation, and the number of in-context demonstrations.

Since the corpus is constructed independently of the queries and lacks query-level relevance annotations, we assess the utility of the retrieved RoTs through downstream ethical judgment performance. As shown in Table 4, IREA performs best across di-verse settings, indicating that its retrieved RoTs pro-vide useful normative guidance even without query-specific gold RoTs. In Table 7, IREA achieves the second-lowest la-tency, reducing it by 53.4 and 56.6% compared with HyDE and Rewrite, respectively. Since RoT_IRs are generated offline and indexed in advance, they add little to online retrieval latency. Even when both intermediate representations are generated on-line, their combined latency remains lower than that of HyDE and Rewrite. These results indicate that IREA provides a favorable trade-off between retrieval effectiveness and efficiency. 

5 Conclusion 

To address the ethical limitations of LLMs, we introduced normative RAG and proposed IREA, an embedding alignment method for normative re-trieval. IREA maps narrative queries and RoTs into a shared situation-behavior representation, reducing representational, semantic, and struc-tural mismatches and enabling more precise embedding-based retrieval. Experimental results show that IREA consistently outperforms existing approaches, highlighting the limitations of factual retrieval methods when applied to normative re-trieval. These findings demonstrate the potential of extending RAG beyond factual knowledge re-trieval to normative reasoning and more socially responsible LLMs. 8Limitations 

In this paper, we propose an approach that enhances normative retrieval through embedding alignment between narratives and RoTs, and demonstrate its potential to mitigate the ethical limitations of LLMs. Nevertheless, IREA still has limitations and open questions that remain to be addressed in fu-ture work. • Expressiveness of the Situation-Behavior Schema. The situation–behavior representa-tion used in IREA is not intended to fully model all ethical problems, but rather to serve as a minimal schema for mapping narratives and RoTs into a shared representation. Infor-mation such as intent, consequences, and rela-tionships between actors can be incorporated into the situation when relevant to the ethical interpretation of the behavior. However, more complex ethical structures, such as conflicts or priority relations among multiple norms, may not be sufficiently captured by these two components. Future work could explore more flexible intermediate representations that in-troduce additional ethical dimensions or dy-namically incorporate the elements required by each problem. • Domain Dependence of IR Generation. The effectiveness of IREA may also depend on how intermediate representations are gener-ated for different ethical domains. Different domains may require slight domain-specific adjustments to the prompt to capture relevant normative information, which may limit the generalizability of a fixed prompt. However, prompt design is not the primary focus of this work. Our focus is instead on using shared in-termediate representations to reduce the repre-sentational gap between narratives and RoTs. Future work could investigate more adaptive prompting or representation-generation strate-gies across a broader range of ethical domains. • Limited Evaluation Setting. The effective-ness of IREA was demonstrated on two dis-tinct datasets addressing universal ethics and cultural ethics, respectively. However, these datasets are insufficient to represent the di-versity of ethical norms. This limitation arises from the scarcity of publicly available datasets that comprehensively cover the wide range of ethical judgment problems that norma-tive retrieval aims to address, resulting in a constrained evaluation setting. Therefore, fu-ture work will explore datasets encompass-ing more diverse norms and ethical judgment scenarios to more systematically assess the generalizability of IREA.

A.3 Sensitivity to In-Context Demonstrations 

To evaluate the sensitivity of IREA to in-context examples, we vary the number of demonstrations from 0 to 5 shots and measure retrieval perfor-mance. For the non-zero shot settings, we construct five independent nested demonstration sets through random sampling, with each set containing a differ-ent collection of demonstrations. Within each set, higher-shot configurations subsume lower-shot con-figurations. The 3-shot setting includes the demon-stration used in the corresponding 1-shot setting, and the 5-shot setting further includes the demon-strations used in the corresponding 3-shot setting. This design enables us to analyze the effect of incre-mentally adding demonstrations while controlling for the demonstrations selected in lower-shot set-tings. As shown in Table 8, IREA exhibits an overall trend of improved performance as the number of demonstrations increases. However, the differences across shot settings are relatively small, indicating that IREA is not highly sensitive to the specific demonstrations used. The 1-shot setting already improves all metrics compared to the 0-shot setting. When increasing the number of shots from 1 to 3, Recall@10 changes only marginally, whereas MRR@10 and nDCG@10 improve further. This suggests that additional demonstrations primarily contribute to ranking refinement by placing the gold RoT at higher ranks, rather than simply in-creasing the likelihood of retrieving the gold RoT within the top 10. Furthermore, increasing the number of demonstra-tions from 3 to 5 shots yields gains across all metrics and achieves the highest average perfor-mance. However, the 5-shot setting also shows the largest standard deviation, suggesting that ad-ditional demonstrations can lead to higher perfor-mance but also introduce greater variability depend-ing on the selected examples. Therefore, we adopt the 3-shot setting as the default configuration, as it provides a favorable balance between performance and stability.

In the first case, IREA retrieves the gold RoT and produces the correct final judgment. In the second case, the gold RoT is not retrieved, but an alternative RoT provides comparable nor-mative guidance, and the model still reaches the correct judgment. This illustrates a limitation of single-gold retrieval metrics, which may underes-timate retrieval utility by treating valid alternative RoTs as retrieval failures. The third case shows that correct retrieval alone is insufficient for accurate downstream judgment. Although the gold RoT is retrieved, the model still 13 makes an incorrect prediction, indicating a failure to apply the retrieved norm appropriately to the story. In the final case, the retrieved RoT is topi-cally relevant but normatively misaligned with the target behavior. As a result, the judgment model receives misleading normative guidance, leading to an incorrect final judgment. Overall, these cases in-dicate that downstream performance in normative RAG depends not only on retrieval quality but also on the model’s ability to identify the target behavior and apply the retrieved norm appropriately. Conse-quently, improvements in retrieval performance do not necessarily yield proportional gains in down-stream judgment performance. 

B.2 Visualization of Embedding Alignment 

Figure 5 illustrates how IREA alleviates the chal-lenges of normative retrieval using four randomly sampled examples from each dataset. In the origi-nal embedding space, normative retrieval requires matching a story, represented as a purple triangle, with its corresponding RoT, represented as a purple circle. However, these paired instances are often po-sitioned far apart, which hinders retrieval based on embedding similarity. IREA addresses this issue by mapping both stories and RoTs into interme-diate representations, shown as red triangles and red circles, respectively. This transformation re-duces the semantic distance between correspond-ing story-RoT pairs and improves the alignment of their embeddings. Thus, the visualization demon-strates that IREA facilitates story-RoT alignment by reconstructing both text types within a shared intermediate representation.
