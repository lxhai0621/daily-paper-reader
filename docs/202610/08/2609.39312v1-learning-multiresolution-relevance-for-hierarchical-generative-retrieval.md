## 摘要
使用语义标识符（SID）的生成式检索会在文档层次结构上做出一系列连续决策。对于同一查询，相关文档可能共享粗粒度前缀，并在更细粒度深度上分叉，且分支模式随查询而变化。这些路径揭示了相关性如何在逐层细化中分布，然而标准的全SID监督将它们视为彼此独立的训练目标。为使这种分配显式化，我们将多分辨率相关性形式化为由单一文档级相关性度量在SID层次结构上诱导出的一致条件分布。我们提出RARS，即分辨率对齐的相关性监督（Resolution-Aligned Relevance Supervision），它利用由此得到的细化级分布来监督共享查询表示。RARS在前缀上聚合文档相关性，并训练一个前缀条件预测器以在兄弟分支之间分配相关性。所有承载相关性的子节点都参与局部竞争，且每个局部损失由到达其父节点的相关性质量加权。该目标训练查询编码器同时捕获相关文档共享的粗粒度结构及其更细粒度的分支分配。该预测器在训练后被丢弃，从而在推理时保留标准的自回归检索。在三个多语言ESCI区域设置上的实验表明，在自回归解码下，相比相匹配的全SID训练，该方法取得了一致提升。在统一检索规则下，RARS也优于分组软目标、解码器软目标和采样树监督。这些增益在不同的标识符结构和相关性定义下依然存在。代码可在以下网址获取：https://github.com/Nevaeh7/RARS

## Abstract
Generative retrieval with semantic identifiers (SIDs) makes successive decisions over a document hierarchy. Relevant documents for the same query may share coarse prefixes and diverge at finer depths, with branching patterns varying across queries. These paths reveal how relevance is distributed across successive refinements, yet standard full-SID supervision treats them as separate training targets. To make this allocation explicit, we formulate multiresolution relevance as consistent conditional distributions induced by a single document-level relevance measure across the SID hierarchy. We introduce \textbf{RARS}, \textbf{R}esolution-\textbf{A}ligned \textbf{R}elevance \textbf{S}upervision, which uses the resulting refinement-level distributions to supervise a shared query representation. RARS aggregates document relevance over prefixes and trains a prefix-conditioned predictor to allocate relevance among sibling branches. All relevance-bearing children participate in local competition, and each local loss is weighted by the relevance mass reaching its parent. This objective trains the query encoder to capture both the coarse structure shared by relevant documents and their finer branch allocations. The predictor is discarded after training, preserving standard autoregressive retrieval at inference. Experiments on three multilingual ESCI locales show consistent improvements over matched full-SID training under autoregressive decoding. RARS also outperforms grouped soft-target, decoder soft-target, and sampled-tree supervision under a common retrieval rule.
