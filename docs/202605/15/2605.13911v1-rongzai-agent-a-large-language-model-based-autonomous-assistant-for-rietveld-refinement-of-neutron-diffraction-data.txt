Abstract 

Neutron diffraction (ND) is an indispensable technique for determining atomic positions (especially light elements) and thus serves as a critical probe for reveal-ing microscopic structures in materials science. However, traditional Rietveld refinement of ND data relies heavily on manual operation of specialized soft-ware, which is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and highly dependent on user expertise, severely hindering automated analysis. To address this challenge, this paper presents the Dr.Sai-Rongzai agent, an autonomous refinement assistant based on a large language model (LLM), a spe-cialist knowledge base, and the GSAS-II refinement engine, achieving for the first time an intelligent refinement that integrates knowledge-driven decision-making. The agent accomplishes a fully automated workflow from natural language task parsing to autonomous decision-making, execution of refinement strategies, and report generation. Evaluation on five representative samples shows that the Rongzai agent achieves lower Rwp values than human specialists on three sam-ples (2.88% vs. 4.42%, 5.06% vs. 5.40%, 7.60% vs. 9.00%), while on the other two samples its results are very close to those of the specialists. The agent is currently deployed at the China Spallation Neutron Source (CSNS) and is open for exter-nal user registration, providing an intelligent and user friendly analytical tool for materials research. This work fully leverages the cutting-edge advantages of LLM, offers a new path to solve the long-standing problem of automated refine-ment, takes a key step toward intelligent and fully automated crystallographic analysis, and holds great potential to accelerate AI for Science discoveries in neutron-based materials characterization.

# 1 Introduction 

Neutron powder diffraction (NPD) is a central probe of crystal and magnetic structure, yet converting diffraction patterns into reliable structural models remains a major practical bottleneck in materials research [1–3]. The difficulty is intrinsic: powder diffraction compresses three-dimensional structural information into a one-dimensional profile [4, 5]. In principle, this inverse problem can be solved by Rietveld refinement, which fits a physically parameterized model to the full diffraction pattern [6, 7]. In practice, however, success depends not only on optimization, but on choosing an appropriate refinement path. Because structural, instrumental and microstructural parameters are often strongly coupled, they must be released in a physically sensi-ble order, with repeated adjustments based on intermediate outcomes [8, 9]. Rietveld refinement is therefore not merely a nonlinear least-squares problem, but a scientific decision process carried out under physical constraints and uncertainty. This depen-dence on expert judgment has become a critical bottleneck for high-throughput and parametric diffraction studies, where large numbers of datasets must be analysed rapidly and consistently [10]. A wide range of automated refinement methods has been developed to address this challenge, including fixed workflows, rule-based expert systems, reinforcement learn-ing, and Bayesian or global optimization strategies [10–15]. Although these approaches have advanced the field, they have not resolved its core limitation: robust refinement requires context-aware, sequential, and corrective decision-making. Predefined work-flows are brittle when confronted with unexpected refinement behaviour; rule-based 2systems are constrained by manually encoded knowledge; and search-based methods can be computationally intensive while still lacking the capacity to interpret interme-diate failures and revise strategy accordingly. As a result, general-purpose autonomous refinement that is both reliable and practical on real experimental data remains out of reach. Recent progress in large language model (LLM)-based agents suggests a different route. By integrating reasoning, planning and tool use in a closed feedback loop, agents can perform tasks that require iterative judgment rather than one-shot prediction [16, 17]. This is precisely the structure of Rietveld refinement: a successful analysis requires evaluating partial fits, deciding what to refine next, identifying when a strategy has become unstable or unphysical, and recovering accordingly. The challenge, therefore, is not simply to automate software execution, but to operationalize expert refinement logic in an autonomous, tool-grounded system. Here we present Dr.Sai-RongZai, the first intelligent agent for autonomous NPD Rietveld refinement that achieves knowledge-driven decision-making by integrating a LLM with specialist refinement knowledge. The work builds on Open Dr.Sai (https://github.com/hepai-lab/drsai), a scientific AI agent framework developed by our team. The agent combines an LLM, specialist refinement knowledge, and a cus-tom GSAS-II execution interface within an iterative decision–execution–evaluation loop (Fig. 1). Given diffraction data and structural inputs, it plans refinement actions, executes them, interprets intermediate outcomes, and updates its strategy until con-vergence. Across multiple representative samples, RongZai agent achieves refinement quality comparable to, and in several cases better than, that of human specialists, as measured by Rwp .

More broadly, this work shows that LLM agents can serve not only as interfaces to scientific tools, but as autonomous operators of expert analytical workflows, opening a practical path toward scalable data analysis in neutron scattering and related fields.

The core of Rongzai Agent consists of three components: a LLM, the GSAS-II refinement engine, and an expert knowledge base. The LLM is responsible for task decomposition and refinement decision-making. GSAS-II, after secondary develop-ment, serves as an automated refinement tool that executes specific tasks. The expert knowledge base encodes the refinement experience accumulated by domain experts into system prompts, guiding the LLM to make professional decisions. The agent employs 4a ”Feedback-Driven Self-Loop” architecture, as shown in Alg. 1, achieving dynamic iterative optimization of refinement parameters through continuous feedback of multi-modal information, including refinement indices and fitting plots. The LLM interacts with the GSAS-II tool through four dedicated code blocks: refine init (initializing the refinement project), refine (executing refinement steps), refine pause (pausing and waiting for user instructions), and refine finish (completing the task and outputting the report), thereby completing the entire workflow from initialization to final report generation. After the user provides instructions and input files, the system automat-ically performs iterative refinement until the results are acceptable. Throughout the entire task, contextual information is fully preserved, ensuring that the LLM can follow long-term strategies. 

## 2.2 The prompts design for LLM 

In LLM application development, prompt design is essential for building effective agents. This section illustrates the principles and best practices of prompt design in the Rongzai agent. First, the LLM is clearly defined as a ”neutron diffraction refine-ment assistant,” establishing its professional boundaries. A three-state machine is then constructed to manage idle, executing, and waiting modes, ensuring appropriate responses based on the current state. The refinement process follows a linear iterative model. The user provides one instruction and three files: a diffraction pattern (.gsa), instrument parameters (.instprm), and crystal information (.cif). The LLM gener-ates a GSAS-II project file (.gpx) to start the task. As shown in Fig. 2, the process enters the COMPLETE state upon the first request, checks data completeness, then switches to RUNNING to execute refinement. After each round, results are evaluated. If improvement is possible, the system saves a backup and adjusts parameters using embedded expert knowledge. When criteria are met or a local optimum is reached, it switches to AWAITING INPUT, presenting results and suggestions to the user. The user may roll back to a previous backup or adjust the strategy. Once accepted, the agent outputs a standardized report with clear markers for downstream parsing. This state-machine and backup mechanism ensures flexibility and traceability. This multi-layered approach, which includes role definition, state management, specialist knowledge, and specific tool handling, forms a reusable framework for prompt design, offering systematic guidance for building domain-specific LLM agents. 

## 2.3 The GSAS-II refinement tool 

GSAS-II is a comprehensive open-source crystallographic analysis software package capable of handling the complete Rietveld refinement workflow. Based on GSAS-II, we have developed an automated refinement tool specifically designed for NPD, achieving full-process automation from project creation, parameter configuration, and refinement execution to result exportation via remote API calls. The tool adopts a FastMCP server architecture, supporting remote calls through the SSE protocol and asynchronous concurrent processing of multiple tasks. Core functionalities include automatic project creation and data import, intelligent cleaning of anomalous values, background parameter optimization, and flexible configuration of various refinement 5Fig. 2 Flowchart of the prompt design and refinement process for Rongzai agent. Upon completion of refinement, the system automati-cally performs data consistency verification, generates high-resolution plots (including observed values, calculated values, background, and Bragg peak positions), and out-puts CSV data, structural parameter text files, and standard CIF format files. # 3 Results and discussion 

## 3.1 Experimental setup

The exper-iment tests the refinement performance of the Rongzai agent on five materials using four different base models. The results are then compared with those of two human spe-cialists in terms of refinement cycles, refinement quality, and time consumption. The four base models employed for Rongzai agent are GPT-4.1 and GPT-5 from OpenAI (https://openai.com), MiniMax-m2.7 from MiniMax (https://www.minimaxi.com), and Scienceone-s1-base-ultra from the ScienceOne team (https://www.scienceone.cn). Specialist II is an instrument scientist at the high-resolution diffraction instrument (TREND) beamline, specializing in neutron diffraction techniques with extensive experience in Rietveld refinement. 3.1.1 Materials and data collection 

Five experimental patterns are used to evaluate the performance of the Rongzai agent: Si, LaB 6, zeolite, Lithium-ion battery cathode (LiNi 0.9 Ni 0.05 Mn 0.05 O2) [18] and Sodium-ion battery cathode (NaNi 0.33 Mn 0.33 Fe 0.33 O2) [19] . The initial crystal structures of these materials have been previously determined. The Si pattern is acquired using the Multi-Physics Instrument (MPI) of CSNS, while the remaining four patterns are acquired using the TREND of CSNS.

## 3.4 Visual comparison of fitted patterns 

Given that these parameters cover the majority of neutron powder diffraction data in the absence of magnetic structure, Rongzai agent is well suited to handle most time-of-flight (TOF) ND Rietveld refinement tasks. Although the Rwp is a valid criterion, scientists also assess the acceptability of a refinement by visually inspecting the fit, as shown in Fig. 5. Fig. 5 compares the fitted patterns of all five samples with the lowest Rwp values achieved by Rongzai agent (using its best-performing base model per sample) and by Specialist I, who possesses extensive refinement experience and provided the expert knowledge base for the agent. For each sample, the left panel shows the agent’s best fit, and the right panel shows the specialist’s best fit. Overall, the fitted patterns are consistent with the Rwp values reported in Table 2. For most samples, both Rongzai agent and Specialist I achieve visually satisfactory fits with well-matched peak posi-tions and intensities. For the NaNi 0.33 Mn 0.33 Fe 0.33 O2 sample (Figs. 5( e1) and ( e2)), the agent achieves a substantially lower Rwp (7.60% vs. 9.00%). However, a closer inspection reveals minor deviations in the agent’s fit, primarily in the peak positions within the low-TOF region, where the specialist’s fit shows better alignment. Human specialists tend to focus on maintaining as many well-fitted peaks as possible, whereas Rongzai appears to pay less attention to this aspect. Despite these local discrepancies, the agent’s overall fit remains acceptable, and the quantitative metric clearly favors the agent. The agent’s occasional minor discrepancies in local peak positions suggest that further improvement could be achieved by expanding the specialist knowledge base or fine-tuning the LLM with more refined refinement strategies. # 4 Conclusion 

In this work, we have developed the Rongzai agent, an intelligent autonomous assis-tant for Rietveld refinement of NPD data, built upon the Open Dr.Sai framework of our team. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first realization of knowledge-driven autonomous refinement using a LLM, where the agent integrates the LLM for task decomposition and decision-making, the GSAS-II tool as the refinement executor, and a specialist knowledge base to guide refinement strategies. Through a feedback-driven self-loop architecture, Rongzai agent achieves fully automated refinement from natural language input to final report generation. Systematic evaluation on five distinct mate-rials yields the following key findings. In terms of refinement quality, Rongzai agent attains the lowest Rwp values on three samples (Zeolite: 2.88%; LiNi 0.9Ni 0.05 Mn 0.05 O2:5.06%; NaNi 0.33 Mn 0.33 Fe 0.33 O2: 7.60%), substantially outperforming both human spe-cialists. For the remaining two samples, its results are very close to those of the best specialist. Overall, the Rongzai agent represents a significant step toward fully automated and intelligent Rietveld refinement, achieving performance comparable to, and in some cases better than, that of human specialists, while offering superior effi-ciency. The agent is currently deployed at the CSNS and is open for external use, providing the materials science community with an efficient, user-friendly tool that tackles a long-standing challenge in crystallography and exemplifies the potential 12 of AI for Science in accelerating data-driven discoveries. The successful demonstra-tion of knowledge-driven decision-making in an autonomous refinement agent opens new avenues for applying LLMs to complex scientific workflows. Although Rongzai agent performs well quantitatively, it occasionally exhibits local underfitting in specific regions (e.g., low TOF regions), indicating room for improvement in controlling local fitting quality — a limitation that can be addressed in future work by expanding the specialist knowledge base or by fine-tuning the LLM with domain-specific refinement knowledge.
